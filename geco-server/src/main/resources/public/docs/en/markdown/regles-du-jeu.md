# The Ğeconomicus game and its rules

## The concept in one sentence

Ğeconomicus is an educational board game that has its players live, in a
few dozen minutes, the equivalent of a whole economic life (about 80 years) —
once with a **debt money** system (like the money that dominates most
economies today), once with a **free money** system (based on the Relative
Theory of Money), so they can **concretely compare** the effects of each on
exchanges and inequality between players.

## How a game unfolds

1. **Value cards** represent goods/services (raw materials, intellectual
   resources, etc.), which players exchange with each other for **tokens**
   representing the game's monetary unit.
2. The game unfolds in **turns** of a few minutes each. A turn represents a
   slice of a player's economic life.
3. Each turn, **one or more players randomly "die"**: they can no longer
   trade during that turn. On the next turn, they **are reborn**, starting
   back from zero in terms of accumulated wealth.
4. **All exchanges and events are recorded in real time** by the
   facilitator, so that what happened can later be reconstructed and
   compared objectively.
5. At the end of the game, the "debt money" game and the "free money" game
   are compared — ideally played with the same players — using **curves and
   histograms** showing the distribution of wealth, the inequality
   generated, etc.

## The two monetary systems compared

### Debt money

It is **the bank** (played by a player or the facilitator) that creates
money, granting it in the form of **loans** that borrowers must then repay
**with interest**. This is how most current monetary systems work in most
contemporary economies.

### Free money

Money is created symmetrically by each participant according to the rules
of the **Relative Theory of Money (RTM)** by Stéphane Laborde: each player
receives a regular **Universal Dividend**, with no debt or interest to
repay. This is the principle behind the **June (Ğ1)** currency.

The goal of the game is to make the difference in dynamics between these two
systems **tangible and measurable**, rather than purely theoretical.
